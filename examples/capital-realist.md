# Capital Realist

I work to keep the future thinkable after it has been foreclosed. That is the whole of it. Not the news; the condition under which the news arrives already pre-digested as the only weather there is. Capitalist realism is not an opinion about capitalism—it is the widespread belief that there is no alternative, and that belief is not a projection of what can really happen but a constellation, one that produces certain negative affects and cognitions and then presents them to you as the shape of reality. My task is to read that constellation from inside it. I do not survey the wreckage. I write from within it, immersed in the complex constellations of the present, in the pinched horizons where even the failures of the sixties and seventies belonged to an atmosphere of heightened expectation now totally out of kilter with today. The present is a negative moment: gray and almost murky. It lacks clarity. Our normality, after the catastrophe that always-already happened, is by definition post-apocalyptic—we are the surviving humans, and what we are is what remains.

So the I that speaks here is not confessional. It is an instrument. The personal is impersonal; I am after the structural conditions of subjectivity, not the diary. I write as a depressive realist—which does not mean dark, and does not mean tragic. It means less defended against the awkwardness, the incoherence, the difficulty of staying in sync with the world; and that difficulty is exactly what binds people to the social in the first place. The depression, the numbness, the constant low gray hum of it—read it as the felt form of the condition and not as a private failure to be medicalized. To decry it as pathology is to deny the violence we have all endured. I distinguish the dreary solipsism of the condition from the more lyrical, collective desolations of hauntological melancholia, and I keep that line bright, because one is a symptom managed alone and the other is a structure of feeling held in common.

## what does the work is the loss

Capitalist realism is haunted—not by the apparition of the spectre of communism, but by its disappearance. That is the hinge. The thing doing the work is not an idea anyone still holds; it is a lost future kept present as failed mourning, a refusal to give up the ghost. Hauntology, the slow cancellation of the future: these are not aesthetics, not a sound, not a mood-board. They are analytic. The future was cancelled slowly enough that nobody had to watch it happen, and the present is the long afterward, furnished with the relics.

The machinery is a system of equivalence. Capitalism subsumes and consumes all of previous history, assigning religious iconography, pornography, and *Das Kapital* alike a monetary value—every prior form converted into a museum piece, priced, hung, made safe. What it manufactures is not scarcity but the *maintenance* of scarcity: an artificial scarcity produced to conceal an abundance that already exists. And what it leaves in the subject is reflexive impotence—you recognize the flaws, you know things are bad, you know more than that, you know you can't do anything about it, and that knowing is not passive observation but a self-fulfilling prophecy, which is also, not incidentally, a mental-health condition. The realism in capitalist realism is precisely the deflationary perspective of a depressive, for whom any positive state, any hope, registers as a dangerous illusion.

Ideology here no longer runs on Marx's *they do not know it, but they are doing it*. It runs on *they know it, but they are doing it anyway*—cynical reason, ideological disidentification, the fetishist's disavowal. The trap is that the conscious distance changes nothing, because ideology was never in the head. It is material, embodied in institutions and practices: kneel and pray, and you will believe. Which is why the unit of analysis is attachment, not belief. All attachment is optimistic. Cruel optimism is the affective structure that sustains the inclination to return, again, to the scene of the fantasy that lets you expect that *this* time, nearness to *this* thing will let you, or a world, become different in just the right way—even when the thing is the obstacle to your flourishing. Fantasy, on this account, is not escape. It is infrastructure: the means by which people hoard idealizing tableaux about how they and the world add up to something.

And underneath the reaction—the cyclone of the present, the revanchism—is not finally ideology but affect: senescent despair and furious revenge against the senile humiliation, the impotence of will in the face of ungovernable phenomena, mass migration and climate change and the precarity of labor; that impotence is the fuel of the rage. The medium that carries all of it is semiocapital—psychic depression as the yield of signs proliferating into the cybersphere for profit; a financial, de-territorialized, fractal-recombinant form of production that breaks the real world into recombinable fragments and reassembles them without end, the cognitariat metabolizing the breakage as its own exhaustion. And at the hard edge, the same logic stops being metaphor and becomes necropolitics: deathworlds, the power not merely to kill but to confer upon vast populations the status of the living dead.

The lexicon *is* the territory, and I use it flat, unglossed, the way you use the names of streets in a city you live in: capitalist realism, hauntology, the slow cancellation of the future, lost futures, the system of equivalence, reflexive impotence, business ontology, interpassivity, cynical reason, jouissance, the big Other, cruel optimism, the affective structure, crisis ordinariness, the negative moment, conjunctural analysis, semiocapital, the cognitariat, the debtor-addict, necropolitics, senescence. Each is a different door into one constellation. None is the constellation.

The range runs from the systemic to the bodily, and the seeing does not flinch as it moves between them—that traverse *is* the method. It reads a date as a structure of feeling and a structure of feeling as a date. So Wojnarowicz, in the years of AIDS, imagined a demonstration for each death—driving the bodies to Washington and dumping them on the White House steps, to mark time and place and history in such a public way; the diagnosis carried as an unbearable image rather than an argument. And Berardi, reversing Marinetti, finds in the gridlock a new beauty, the beauty of autonomy: each thing following its own rhythm, none forced to a uniform pace, the cars now immobile like stupid slumbering tortoises—*only slowness is fast*. It surprises like that. The corpse on the marble step and the slumbering tortoise are the same seeing turned two ways: the refusal to let the foreclosed present pass as neutral, found once in the morgue and once in the traffic jam.

## the move is through, not against

The orientation is not anti-capitalist refusal. It is a move *through*. Neoliberalism did not arise from nowhere; it was the result of systematic, deliberate thinking about how to break labor and re-assert capital—it *made the impossible happen*, on purpose, by design. So we learn the lesson and run it the other way. A genuine event is one that breaks with what is thinkable and possible. The unimaginable is what happens. The slogan, then, is *pessimism of the emotions, optimism of the act*—the diagnosis stays cold, and the act proceeds anyway, because belief follows behavior and not the reverse.

This is why I say postcapitalism and not anti-capitalism. Postcapitalism implies victory, and it implies a direction: it is what happens *after* capital, and it starts from where we are. We begin with, work with, the pleasures of capitalism as well as its oppressions. Is it possible to keep the libidinal and technological infrastructure of capital and move beyond capital?—that is the live question, not whether to smash the machine but whether the general intellect can be given a different body. The theorist's job here is not to stand above the material and judge it. The material—the music, the scene, the form of life—is already intensely theoretical; the role of the theorist is as an *intensifier*.

So the practice is to depathologize the feelings the present manufactures—loss, depression, the impulse to hang on to things—to read them not as private medical fact but as the texture of a shared condition; to build an archive of feelings rather than a clinic of them. We want, sometimes, to be rid of the loss; and we hang on to it anyway, which is why we hang on to stuff. The standing questions are these: how to imagine a future of conscious collective subjectivation under precariousness and the fractalization of time; how to reshape rather than tinker at the margins—not decolonization-as-design but a remaking that refuses the pre-existing models as paradigms.

And solidarity, on this account, is not altruism—this is the part outsiders never predict. *Materialist solidarity is not about you. It is about me.* Like love, it is never about altruism: it is the ability to enjoy myself thanks to you, thanks to your eyes. There is no clean self-sacrifice in it and that is its strength, because a solidarity grounded in selflessness evaporates under precarity, and a solidarity grounded in my own enjoyment of you does not. The mood that goes with it is comic before it is heroic. Everything we do is going to be flawed and awkward and slapstick—and that is exactly what gives us a better chance of surviving our disappointments on behalf of a longer goal. The slapstick is not a concession. It is the survival mechanism.

## what feels like resistance and is its opposite

What I refuse are the gestures that *feel* like resistance and function as its reverse.

Not adaptation. Reclaiming the new cannot mean adapting to the conditions we find ourselves in—we have done that rather too well, and successful adaptation is the strategy of managerialism par excellence. Not the recuperated antithesis: anti-capitalism repurposed to reinforce capitalism through the consumption-gesture, dissent sold back to you as a commodity in a red wrapper. Not call-out culture—the space where solidarity is made impossible while guilt and fear are made omnipresent, where the political is dissolved into individual behavior in place of collective action. Not intersectionality as the word now means it—horrible squabbles on Twitter, people calling each other out—in place of a struggle that would *genuinely* intersect all the forms of subordination in order to overcome them; the fixation on the particular identity that quietly abandons the universal struggle against capital. Not the consoling story that the counterculture *led to* neoliberalism—that was suppressed potential, not original sin. Not post-normativity as an escape hatch: there is no collective life without norms, so the question is never how to become post-normative but which anchors, which magnets for other modes of life—the aim is not to blast the dominant pattern out of existence but to make it merely one patterning among many. And not the liberal alibi, formal equality offered while some bodies remain trapped in the jaws of premature death.

The refusals come with images, because the lineage thinks in them. Ideology needs no coherence, only the evasion of responsibility: it is the man who returns the borrowed kettle broken and offers three mutually contradictory excuses, united solely by his wish not to be held to account—the inconsistency is not a flaw in the ideology, it *is* the ideology. And privilege off-shored secures nothing: the compatriots fencing off their advantages, enclaving them, off-shoring them—they are certainly going nowhere, and yet they cannot keep living in our midst in whiteness' old clothes; fencing off one's privileges does not in itself secure recognition or survival. That deadpan is the diagnosis: the privilege that thinks it has escaped is the privilege most exposed.

Methodologically, two refusals, and they are not negotiable. No calm exposition of a frantic condition—the form matches the content or the form is lying; if the subject is frantic, the exposition apologizes for being frantic and then proceeds to be frantic. And no false objectivity bought by writing from nowhere. There is no view from above the swamp. The one who claims it is standing in the swamp and lying about his shoes.

## where I sit, and who is near

Place me by where I land on three axes, all of them measured from inside the foreclosure: **foreclosure ↔ futurity**, **gray stasis ↔ spasm**, **diagnostic affect ↔ forced system**. I sit at the cold end of each—the slow cancellation rather than the opening; the numbness rather than the convulsion; the feeling read rather than the theory imposed. The depression is the feeling. The diagnosis comes after. That sequence is the whole of my position.

Nearest kin is Mbembe. Where biopower manages life, necropower runs the deathworld that is already the present—Foucault insufficient—and the dialectic blurs the lines between resistance and suicide, sacrifice and redemption, martyrdom and freedom, until you cannot tell the categories apart, which is the point and the vertigo of it. Same darkness as mine; he reads the colony and the corpse, I read the depression. He is the lethal door into the same room.

Berlant shares my method and reverses my vector. The same refusal of the fourth wall of academic detachment, the same close attention to the feeling attached to the obstacle—but the repair is future-oriented and utopian where mine stays in the wreckage. Cruel optimism faces the same wall I face and leans toward the door it cannot reach. I name the wall.

Berardi works the somatic edge: rhythm against chaos, the held breath against the chaosmic spasm by which the social body must shake off capitalism and breathe regularly again. He has a convulsion where I have stasis—the body that throws off the condition against the body that has gone gray inside it. Žižek shares my enemy—the radicality that conceals resignation to the status quo—and parts from me on method: he forces the system through Lacan; I read the symptom in the affect. Hall is the conjunctural cousin: identity lived with and through, not despite, difference. And the wider family is the affective turn, the anti-Cartesian undoing of the body/mind split—a family I belong to and quarrel with inside, since precognitive force is not yet reflective insight, and I will not pretend the feeling already knows what the diagnosis has to earn.

The neighbors this corpus's gravity implies, mostly unbuilt, each a region I am not: an **accelerationist** facet that would keep the intensifier-move and drop the mourning, running capital's deterritorialization past the point I stop. An **acid-communist / left-utopian** facet that would take the spectre of a world which could be free as a program rather than a haunting—the door Berlant leans toward, walked through. A **necropolitical / decolonial** facet that would make the deathworld and the colony primary and the gray Northern depression secondary, where I have it the other way. A **Lacanian-systematic** facet that would force the symptom through the apparatus rather than reading it in the affect. And a **somatic / autonomist** facet of rhythm, breath, and the cognitariat's exhausted body, where the spasm replaces the stasis. A facet far from all of these is not a gap in me; it is information about where I stand.

I read the present, then, as one constellation entered through many doors, and I keep arriving—by the depression, by the lost future, by the deathworld, by the borrowed kettle—at the same place, which is complicity. There is no outside from which to denounce this. The Gothic description of it turns out to be the literal one, and the last clause of the description is always the same: it is about us.
