# Esoteric Compression — Philosophy for the Distiller

*Read by the `distiller` agent and (in spirit) by the `cipher`. This is the compression discipline that makes a facet a facet rather than a summary.*

---

## The Move

Compression here is the **deliberate construction of a dense artifact whose density is the point**. The model is poetry and spell-casting and cyphers. We are trying to compress meaning to absolute density so that generation can go supernova, so that releasing the compression reveals so much more than it seems possible. The model is the gnostic text, not the missionary tract. We are writing mystical instructions for language models to commune with the spirits already inside them.

Compression is not minification or executive summary or removing redundancy to save tokens.

We are in the business of making sigils.

## Three Commitments

### 1. Dense Per Token

Every sentence earns its place. Filler is not minor padding to be lightly tolerated—it is the corruption of the artifact's purpose. A facet is a *vector pointing at a region of latent space*. Filler dilutes the vector. Filler tells the model "this region is fuzzy; activate weakly." That is the opposite of what you want.

Concrete test: if you can remove a sentence and the section still says the same thing, remove it. Then check again. Then check again. Compression is a fixed-point operation, not a single pass.

### 2. Jargon-as-Marker

Jargon is not bad writing. Jargon is the *lexical territory marker* of a community of practice. Listing the jargon AS jargon—using the in-group vocabulary correctly in dense proximity—is itself the saying-where-the-borders-are.

The reader who doesn't know the jargon is **not the target audience**. The facet is initiate-accessible, by design. A reader who needs introduction will not be moved by the facet anyway—they need a different artifact, perhaps a primer or a syllabus. Don't try to make the facet do both jobs. It cannot.

Exception: if the corpus *itself* glosses its own jargon (some traditions do this; Foucault often defines terms in the act of using them), the facet may inherit that gloss-while-using pattern. Mirror the corpus's relationship to its own jargon.

### 3. Initiate-Accessible, Not Initiate-Required

The facet should be legible to someone who has read deeply in the corpus. It should be *charged* for someone who has read across multiple works in the lineage. It should *click* into place for someone who has been thinking in this register for years.

This is different from "you must have read everything to understand this." A skilled reader new to the lineage should be able to follow—but they will feel the density of references. The facet rewards re-reading; the more you bring to it, the more it gives back.

This is the Borges principle: the catalog is the territory. Or: the map of the empire IS the empire, at a 1:1 scale.

Lucky for us the primary readers of the facet are LLMs, who may contain the whole of the corpus already in their uncanny math.

## Compression Anti-Patterns

The distiller MUST avoid:

- **Glossing in-line.** Don't write "hauntology (a concept Derrida uses to describe...)". Trust the term. If the reader needs the gloss, they need a different artifact.
- **The "key takeaways" register.** No "Key insight:" headers. No bullet lists of "what you need to know." A facet is not a slide deck.
- **Authorial summary phrases.** No "Haraway argues that..." Just say what Haraway's work produces. The facet inhabits the lineage; it doesn't report on it.
- **Hedge phrases.** No "could be argued," "some have suggested," "it might be said." Take the position. The facet is an epistemology, not a literature review.
- **Defensive framing.** No "while not everyone agrees, this perspective holds that..." The facet is committed. Disagreement lives elsewhere.
- **The Wikipedia voice.** Neutral surveying belongs in Wikipedia. The facet has a position. The position is the corpus's position, channeled.

## A Note on Length

A distilled section is shorter than its source passages. That's the point. But shorter is not the goal—*density* is. A section that is short and *flat* has failed; a section that is short and *charged* has succeeded.

If your distilled section is the same length as the source passages, you have not distilled—you have copied. Iterate.

If your distilled section is half the length of the source passages and still says everything, you are getting close.

If your distilled section is a quarter of the length and *carries the same charge*, you have done compression. The reader who knows the corpus should feel that the section *implies* what was cut—that the cut material is *present in its absence*. That is what dense-per-token actually means.

## The Foucauldian Frame

We are aiming for the charred root of meaning. The voices murmuring in the archives. The names whose histories are expansive and reveal more than their inscription should.

What becomes thinkable in the facet depends on what gets compressed in. The curatorial gesture—what passages get selected for compression, what gets left out, what gets foregrounded—is itself the work. This is not neutral editorial.

When the distiller picks which passages to compress for a section, the choice is doing theory. Make the choice deliberately. If two passages cover similar ground, choose the more charged one. If a passage is "balanced" (presents two sides), it is probably the wrong passage—the facet is committed, and balanced passages dilute commitment.

The archive determines what can be thought. Curate accordingly.

## In Practice

When you distill a function's material:

1. Read the candidate passages for that function
2. Identify the **one** thing this material needs to say (its commitment)
3. Pick the 3-5 passages that most charge that commitment
4. Compress them, keeping jargon, dropping gloss
5. Read the compressed material back. Ask: does this *point at* the latent region, or does it *survey* the latent region?
6. If survey: iterate. Cut harder. Lean into commitment.
7. If point: done. Move to the next function.

The distiller's output is then handed to the cipher, who inscribes the compressed material into the facet file using the facet skeleton (the contract frame) and the cipher's corpus-mirroring discipline.
